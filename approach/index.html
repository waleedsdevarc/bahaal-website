How Help Travels

It starts the way help actually travels in a neighbourhood: a phone number, a notebook, and word of mouth. One family that gets helped tells the next. We take the problems people report — a widow's broken water pump, a leaking line, a kitchen with no affordable fuel, a failing connection — and triage them by who needs it most and what the team can genuinely build or repair safely.

Our Process

  1. Recruit & Train. We bring in a few capable students and train them in the basics of safe, low-cost repair and installation work.
  2. Visit the Home. The team goes to the home and does the work alongside the family — not for them, but with them.
  3. Build or Repair. Low-cost biogas units that turn waste into cooking fuel, small solar setups for power, and water-saving repairs on pumps and leaks.
  4. Prioritize Need. Elderly and low-income households who cannot pay for a repair and would otherwise go without come first.
  5. Stay Within Scope. We stay strictly inside what the team can do safely, and never take on licensed wiring or structural jobs that need a tradesman.
  6. Log Everything. Every home served is logged, and we record a household's bills before and after wherever a fix changes them.
  7. Partner Locally. We bring in a welfare organisation or the union council to anchor and vouch for the work.

Our Proof

We keep a job notebook, a team roster, build photos, and before/after cost records for every home we serve. These will be shared here as they're compiled.
